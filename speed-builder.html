Speed Builder: a tempo trainer for music practice

Speed Builder is the part of DrillTime that takes you gradually from a comfortable start tempo to a goal tempo. It is for students who can perform a passage slowly but lose it at full speed, and for teachers who want that progress to be steady.

Who it helps

Every week a student tells me a passage is fine slowly but falls apart at full tempo. Speed Builder is made for that gap. It helps:

  • Students learning scales, studies or fast passages.
  • Anyone who has been stuck at the same tempo for weeks.
  • Exam candidates who need to reach a marked tempo.
  • Teachers who want to set a clear tempo goal for the week.

How to use it

Set a start tempo you can manage cleanly and a goal tempo you want to reach, then start. Speed Builder raises the tempo in small steps, so each step feels only slightly harder than the last.

  1. Choose the passage. Short is better: one phrase or a few bars.
  2. Set the start tempo where the passage is clean and relaxed.
  3. Set the goal tempo, often the marked tempo of the piece.
  4. Start, and keep repeating the passage. The screen shows the current tempo, which step you are on and the time left.
  5. If a step falls apart, pause, then start again from a slower start tempo.

Practice tips from a teacher

Speed grows out of accuracy. When students force the tempo up, tension gets locked into their hands, and the gradual climb is what fixes it. ABRSM’s practice advice for students (opens in a new tab) gives the same advice: get the rhythm right at a slow tempo, then gradually raise the metronome until you reach the correct tempo.

  • Keep the goal realistic for one session. A modest jump above your start tempo is plenty.
  • Stay relaxed. If your shoulders rise or your grip tightens, the tempo is too high.
  • Aim for a clean repetition at each step before you think about the next one.
  • Expect a plateau now and then, and come back the next day. A tempo that felt impossible often feels normal after a break.

A sample speed-building plan

A sample five-day plan for one passage
StepTempo approachGoal
Day 1Start well below the marked tempo, with the goal a little higherClean, relaxed repetitions at every step
Day 2Same start, goal slightly higher than day 1The same accuracy at the new top tempo
Day 3Start a few BPM higher than day 1Notice where tension creeps in, and slow down there
Day 4A short session with the same settings as day 3Consolidate, nothing new
Day 5Goal set to the marked tempoReach it cleanly once, then stop for the day

Key terms

Tempo
The speed of the music, measured in beats per minute (BPM).
Clean repetition
One complete pass through a passage at a set tempo, with the right notes, the right rhythm and no tension.
Plateau
A stretch of practice where the tempo you can manage stops rising for a while. It is normal and usually passes with patient, regular practice.

Questions

How is Speed Builder different from a normal metronome?

A metronome stays at one tempo until you change it. Speed Builder changes it for you, step by step, from your start tempo to your goal tempo, so you can keep your attention on the music.

How big should the gap between the start and goal tempo be?

For one session, a modest gap works best. If the last steps fall apart, lower the goal and build up again over several days.

Can teachers use Speed Builder in lessons?

Yes. It needs no account and works offline, so a teacher can set a start and goal tempo on a shared iPad during the lesson.
